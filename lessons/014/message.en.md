🇪🇸 *Lesson 14 — Language Transfer*

*The core rule:*
The verb _saber_ \(to know\) is irregular for the "I" form: _sé_ instead of the expected _sabo_\. The rest of the forms follow regular patterns\. Spanish also has pronouns for he \(_él_\) and she \(_ella_\), but you don't need to use them unless you want to emphasize or clarify who you're talking about — the verb ending already tells you\.

*New constructions:*
\- _sé_ — I know \(irregular, from _saber_\)
\- _sabe_ — he/she/it knows, you formal know
\- _lo sé_ — I know it \(common way to say "I know"\)
\- _él_ — he
\- _ella_ — she
\- _salgo_ — I leave, I go out \(from _salir_, irregular with G\)
\- _sale_ — he/she/it leaves, you formal leave
\- _salen_ — they leave, you plural leave
\- _sales_ — you informal leave
\- _tengo que_ — I have to, I must
\- _cuando_ — when
\- _poner_ — to put
\- _pongo_ — I put
\- _suponer_ — to suppose
\- _supongo_ — I suppose

*Remember:*
When you add _que_ after _tengo_, it shifts the meaning from simple possession \("I have"\) to obligation \("I have to"\)\. _Tengo que_ is everyday obligation; _debo_ is more serious\.

*Examples:*
\- _No sé qué quiere_ — I don't know what he wants
\- _Salgo a comer_ — I'm going out to eat
\- _¿Cuándo vienes a comer?_ — When are you coming to eat?
\- _Tengo que verla_ — I have to see her
\- _Supongo que sí_ — I suppose so
\- _No quiero invitarlo pero ella sí_ — I don't want to invite him but she does
\- _Tengo que comer ahora_ — I have to eat now
