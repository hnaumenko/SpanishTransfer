🇪🇸 *Lesson 24 — Language Transfer*

*Main idea:*
The verb _dar_ \(to give\) works like the other verbs you know\. The pattern is regular: _doy_ \(I give\), _da_ \(he/she gives\), _dan_ \(they give\)\. When you want to say "they give it to me," Spanish just says "they give me it" — the personal pronoun _me_ comes first, then _lo_ or _la_\. And like other verbs, you can drop the future tense if the context is clear: instead of "I will give," just say "I give it to you tomorrow\."

*New constructions:*
\- _dar_ — to give
\- _doy_ — I give
\- _da_ — he/she gives, you \(formal\) give
\- _dan_ — they give, you guys give
\- _ustedes_ — you guys \(plural formal\)

*Remember:*
When you want to say "are you giving it to me tomorrow?" in Spanish, just work out who's doing what and to whom\. Complex English sentences often become simple in Spanish once you identify the action and the people involved\.

*Examples:*
\- _Me dan algo_ — They give me something
\- _Me lo dan_ — They give it to me
\- _Te lo doy mañana_ — I give it to you tomorrow \(I'll give it to you tomorrow\)
\- _¿Me lo da mañana?_ — Are you giving it to me tomorrow?
\- _¿Ustedes nos lo dan mañana?_ — Will you guys give it to us tomorrow?
\- _Quiero dártelo_ — I want to give it to you
