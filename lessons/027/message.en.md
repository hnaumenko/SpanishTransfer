🇪🇸 *Lesson 27 — Language Transfer*

*Key pattern:*
In Spanish, adjectives come after the noun they describe, not before\. You don't say "the global situation" — you say "the situation global\." So the describing word follows what it describes\. There's some flexibility for emphasis, but the standard position is after the noun\.

*New constructions:*
\- _la situación_ — the situation
\- _global_ — global
\- _cámara_ — camera
\- _digital_ — digital
\- _coche_ — car \(masculine\)
\- _caro_ — car \(masculine, spelled with two Rs\)
\- _auto_ — car \(masculine, from automobile\)
\- _máquina_ — machine, car \(feminine\)
\- _grande_ — big
\- _encontrar_ — to find, to encounter
\- _quedar_ — to remain
\- _quedarse_ — to stay \(literally: to remain oneself\)
\- _en_ — in
\- _rojo_ — red

*Remember:*
Even though saying "I can't find it" is longer than "I don't find it," the first is often a shortcut in thought because you use _puedo_ all the time\. Once you're communicative, your speech will adjust naturally as you notice how people actually talk\.

*Examples:*
\- _La situación global_ — The global situation
\- _Una cámara digital_ — A digital camera
\- _Quiero comprar una cámara digital_ — I want to buy a digital camera
\- _El coche grande_ — The big car
\- _Las casas grandes_ — The big houses
\- _Me quedo en el auto rojo_ — I'm staying in the red car
\- _Me he quedado en el coche rojo_ — I have stayed in the red car
\- _He comprado un carro rojo_ — I have bought a red car
