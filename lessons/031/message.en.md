🇪🇸 *Lesson 31 — Language Transfer*

*Key pattern:*
Spanish words that start with _st\-_, _sp\-_, or _s\+consonant_ have an extra _e\-_ at the beginning\. You can't begin a word with those consonant clusters in Spanish the way you can in English\. This is why "state" becomes _estado_, "student" becomes _estudiante_, and "special" becomes _especial_\. It's the same pattern that links "state" to the verb _estar_\.

*New constructions:*
\- _ser_ — to be \(for characteristics\)
\- _soy_ — I am \(from _ser_\)
\- _es_ — he is, she is, you are \(formal\), it is
\- _son_ — they are, you guys are
\- _somos_ — we are
\- _eres_ — you are \(informal\)
\- _él_ — he
\- _ella_ — she
\- _ellos_ — they \(masculine or mixed\)
\- _ellas_ — they \(feminine\)
\- _nosotros_ — we \(masculine or mixed\)
\- _nosotras_ — we \(feminine\)
\- _ustedes_ — you guys
\- _humano_ — human
\- _el humano_ — the human
\- _ser humano_ — a human being
\- _estudiante_ — student
\- _español_ — Spanish
\- _España_ — Spain
\- _escuela_ — school
\- _especial_ — special
\- _espacial_ — spatial
\- _estricto_ — strict
\- _espectacular_ — spectacular
\- _cómo_ — how

*Remember:*
When someone asks what a person is like, you're describing a characteristic, so you use _ser_\. When you ask how someone is doing, that's a state, so you use _estar_\.

*Examples:*
\- _Soy estudiante_ — I am a student
\- _Es posible_ — It is possible
\- _¿Cómo está él?_ — How is he doing?
\- _¿Cómo es él?_ — What is he like?
\- _¿Cómo es ella?_ — What is she like?
\- _Somos estudiantes_ — We are students
\- _Eres especial_ — You are special
