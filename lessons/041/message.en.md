🇪🇸 *Lesson 41 — Language Transfer*

*What this lesson is about:*
Spanish builds both future tenses \(_will_ and _would_\) by adding endings directly onto the full verb\. The pattern comes from _haber_ \(to have\)\. For _will_, you use the sounds from _he_, _has_, _ha_, _hemos_, _han_ — but without the silent H\. For _would_, the key sound is _ía_, which works for I, he, she, it, and you formal\. Both futures stress the ending, pushing the accent forward — a feature the teacher calls "pushing the accent into the future\."

*New constructions:*
\- _bailar_ — to dance \(related to ballerina, which means dancer in Spanish\)
\- _bailaré_ — I will dance
\- _bailaría_ — I would dance
\- _ir_ — to go
\- _iría_ — I would go
\- _comer_ — to eat
\- _comería_ — I would eat
\- _transformar_ — to transform
\- _contaminar_ — to contaminate
\- _imaginar_ — to imagine

*Remember:*
When you want to express "it gets contaminated" or "it transforms itself," Spanish uses the reflexive construction \(_se contamina_\) because Spanish doesn't have the "gets" structure that English uses\.

*Examples:*
\- _Bailamos más tarde_ — We dance later
\- _Vamos a bailar más tarde_ — We're going to dance later
\- _Bailaríamos_ — We would dance
\- _Comería pero no tengo tiempo_ — I would eat but I don't have time
\- _Transformaría la situación_ — It would transform the situation
\- _Se contaminaría_ — It would get contaminated
\- _Imaginaría que no quiere ir_ — I would imagine that he doesn't want to go
