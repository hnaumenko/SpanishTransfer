🇪🇸 *Lesson 42 — Language Transfer*

*The core rule:*
Spanish uses _gustar_ \(to be pleasing\) where English uses "to like\." When you say "I like it" in English, Spanish thinks "it is pleasing to me\." This means the subject and object flip: what you like becomes the subject doing the pleasing, and you become the person receiving that pleasure\. So _me gusta_ literally means "it pleases me," not "I like\."

*New constructions:*
\- _gustar_ — to be pleasing, to please
\- _me gusta_ — it pleases me \(I like it\)
\- _me gustan_ — they please me \(I like them\)
\- _nos gustaría_ — it would please us \(we would like\)
\- _podría_ — I/he/she/it could, you formal could
\- _podrían_ — they could, you plural could
\- _podríamos_ — we could
\- _podrías_ — you informal could
\- _decir_ — to say, to tell
\- _mi casa_ — my house
\- _si_ — if

*Remember:*
Common verbs like _poder_ change more over time\. When forming the conditional, _podría_ drops the middle vowel instead of keeping the full infinitive\.

*Examples:*
\- _Me gustaría comer ahora_ — I would like to eat now
\- _Nos gustaría comer ahora_ — We would like to eat now
\- _Me gustaría verte_ — I would like to see you
\- _¿Podrías venir a mi casa?_ — Could you come to my house?
\- _¿Podrías decirme algo?_ — Could you tell me something?
\- _¿Podrías decirme si quieres venir?_ — Could you tell me if you want to come?
\- _Me gustan_ — I like them
