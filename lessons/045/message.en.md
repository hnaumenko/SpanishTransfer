🇪🇸 *Lesson 45 — Language Transfer*

*What this lesson is about:*
Spanish handles verbs like _gustar_ \(to like\), _interesar_ \(to interest\), and _parecer_ \(to seem\) differently than English\. Instead of saying "I like it," you say "it is pleasing to me\." This means you use _le_ and _les_ \(to him/her/them\) instead of _lo_, _la_, _los_, _las_\. The thing being liked is the subject, and the person doing the liking receives the action\.

*New constructions:*
\- _gustar_ — to be pleasing to \(not "to like" directly\)
\- _le_ — to him, to her, to you \(formal\)
\- _les_ — to them, to you guys
\- _interesar_ — to be interesting to
\- _parecer_ — to seem
\- _bailar_ — to dance
\- _salir_ — to leave or to go out
\- _poner_ — to put
\- _componer_ — to compose \(with putting\)
\- _suponer_ — to suppose
\- _oponer_ — to oppose
\- _sal_ — salt
\- _un poco_ — a little
\- _más_ — more

*Remember:*
With _gustar_, _interesar_, and _parecer_, translate them as "to be pleasing to," "to be interesting to," and "to seem" so the structure with _le_ and _les_ makes sense in your mind\.

*Examples:*
\- _Nos gusta_ — It is pleasing to us / We like it
\- _Le gusta_ — It is pleasing to her/him / She/he likes it
\- _Les gustan_ — They are pleasing to them / They like them
\- _¿Te interesa bailar esta noche?_ — Does it interest you to dance tonight? / Are you interested in dancing tonight?
\- _Me parece que no quiere salir esta noche_ — It seems to me that he doesn't want to go out tonight
\- _No me parece bien_ — It doesn't seem good to me
\- _Quisiera ponerle un poco más de sal_ — I would like to put a little more salt on it
