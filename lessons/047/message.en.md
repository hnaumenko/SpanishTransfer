🇪🇸 *Lesson 47 — Language Transfer*

*Key pattern:*
When a verb's action isn't happening *to* something else — when something breaks by itself, burns by itself, or sells itself — Spanish uses the reflexive structure with _se_\. You say "it breaks itself" where English would say "it breaks" or "it gets broken"\. This structure wraps up the sentence so you're not left waiting to hear what got broken\. Without _se_, Spanish expects you to say what was affected\.

*New constructions:*
\- _la cena_ — the dinner
\- _cenar_ — to dine, to have dinner
\- _quemar_ — to burn
\- _se_ — itself, himself, herself, themselves \(used to show the action affects the subject\)
\- _el sol_ — the sun
\- _romper_ — to break \(related to rupture\)
\- _todo el tiempo_ — all the time
\- _esta noche_ — tonight
\- _este_ — this \(masculine\)
\- _estos_ — these \(masculine\)
\- _ese_ — that \(masculine\)
\- _esos_ — those \(masculine\)
\- _cambiar_ — to change

*Remember:*
The pattern works both ways: _el sol me quema_ \(the sun burns me\) is complete without _se_ because "me" tells you what gets burned\. But _me quemo_ \(I burn myself / I get burnt\) needs the reflexive because otherwise you'd be waiting to hear what you burn\.

*Examples:*
\- _Cenaba_ — I was having dinner / he was having dinner / you were having dinner
\- _Él quemaba la cena_ — He was burning the dinner
\- _La cena se quemaba_ — The dinner was burning \(getting burned\)
\- _Me quemo en el sol_ — I get burnt in the sun
\- _Se rompe todo el tiempo_ — It breaks all the time
\- _Esos se rompen todo el tiempo_ — Those ones break all the time
\- _Este coche_ — This car
\- _Esos coches_ — Those cars
