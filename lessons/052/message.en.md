🇪🇸 *Lesson 52 — Language Transfer*

*Main idea:*
When "to" means "in order to," Spanish requires _para_ before the verb\. You can't just use the infinitive like in English\. So "I'm here to see you" becomes _Estoy aquí para verte_, not just _Estoy aquí verte_\. This is why Spanish speakers learning English sometimes say "I am here for see you" — they're bringing _para_ across from Spanish\.

*New constructions:*
\- _para_ \+ infinitive — in order to \(when "to" implies purpose\)
\- _estar_ \+ \-_ando_/_iendo_ — to be doing something right now \(present continuous\)
\- _\-ante_ \(for \-_ar_ verbs\) / _\-iente_ \(for \-_er_/_ir_ verbs\) — \-ing as an adjective \(e\.g\. interesting, frustrating\)
\- _explicar_ — to explain
\- _tomar_ — to take
\- _un curso_ — a course
\- _salud_ — health
\- _saludable_ — healthy
\- _nadar_ — to swim
\- _interesante_ — interesting
\- _irritar_ — to irritate
\- _irritante_ — irritating
\- _frustrar_ — to frustrate
\- _frustrante_ — frustrating
\- _menguar_ — to wane
\- _menguante_ — waning
\- _un ser_ — a being
\- _un ser viviente_ — a living being
\- _salir_ — to go out, to leave
\- _saliente_ — salient, protruding

*Remember:*
Spanish doesn't use the \-_ando_/\-_iendo_ form after prepositions or to make subjects\. Instead of "swimming is healthy," say _nadar es saludable_ — "to swim is healthy\."

*Examples:*
\- _Hemos hablado para resolverlo_ — We have spoken to resolve it
\- _Hemos hablado para intentar resolverlo_ — We have spoken to try to resolve it
\- _Estoy aquí para verte_ — I'm here to see you
\- _Te he llamado para hablar de ello_ — I have called you to speak about it
\- _Estoy tomando un curso de español_ — I'm taking a Spanish course
\- _Estaba comiendo_ — I was eating
\- _Bailar es saludable_ — Dancing is healthy
\- _Es interesante_ — It is interesting
