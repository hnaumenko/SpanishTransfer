🇪🇸 *Lesson 54 — Language Transfer*

*The core rule:*
Spanish uses the same verb form that comes after _haber_ \(like _vendido_, _cerrado_, _perdido\) as an adjective, just as we do in English with "sold," "closed," or "lost\." The difference is that after _haber_ it never changes, but when it acts as an adjective it has to agree with the noun it describes\. These adjectives almost always use _estar_, not _ser_, with just a few exceptions\.

*New constructions:*
\- _cerrado/a_ — closed \(from _cerrar_, to close\)
\- _perdido/a_ — lost \(from _perder_, to lose\)
\- _terminado/a_ — finished \(from _terminar_, to finish\)
\- _vendido/a_ — sold \(from _vender_, to sell\)
\- _cansado/a_ — tired \(from _cansar_, to tire\)
\- _mojado/a_ — wet \(from _mojar_, to wet\)
\- _es una persona cerrada_ — he/she is a closed person \(structure to avoid gender ambiguity\)

*Remember:*
When you say something _is_ a noun \(like "it is a chair"\), you always use _ser_, even if you add an adjective that would normally take _estar_\. You're naming what it is first — the adjective is just secondary information\.

*Examples:*
\- _La puerta está cerrada_ — The door is closed
\- _Estoy perdida_ — I am lost \(feminine speaker\)
\- _La película está terminada_ — The movie is finished
\- _Están vendidas_ — They are sold \(feminine plural\)
\- _Estoy cansado_ — I am tired
\- _La silla está mojada_ — The chair is wet
\- _Es una silla mojada_ — It is a wet chair
\- _Es una persona cerrada a la idea_ — He is a person closed to the idea
