🇪🇸 *Lesson 58 — Language Transfer*

*The core rule:*
Spanish has a preterite tense for completed past actions — what we express with \-ed in English or irregular forms like "ate" or "ran\." For \-ar verbs, you drop the \-ar and add \-é for "I" and \-ó for "he/she/it/you formal\." The accent always falls on these endings\. So _hablar_ becomes _hablé_ \(I spoke\) and _habló_ \(he/she/it spoke\)\. This tense is less predictable than others, but you can use _hablar_ as your anchor to remember the pattern\.

*New constructions:*
\- _hablé_ — I spoke
\- _habló_ — he/she/it/you formal spoke
\- _compré_ — I bought
\- _compró_ — he/she/it/you formal bought
\- _enviar_ — to send
\- _envié_ — I sent
\- _envió_ — he/she/it/you formal sent
\- _todavía_ — yet, still
\- _ayer_ — yesterday
\- _olvidar_ — to forget
\- _olvidé_ — I forgot
\- _olvidó_ — he/she/it/you formal forgot

*Remember:*
When you want to say "it was sent" in Spanish, think mechanically: "it sent itself\." That gives you _se envió_, without needing _lo_\. Don't follow your intuition to add extra pronouns — work through the literal structure\.

*Examples:*
\- _Compré_ — I bought
\- _Lo compró para mí_ — He bought it for me
\- _No te lo envié todavía_ — I didn't send it to you yet
\- _Lo envió ayer_ — He sent it yesterday
\- _Se envió ayer_ — It was sent yesterday
\- _No sé por qué no habló conmigo_ — I don't know why he didn't speak with me
\- _Iba a hacerlo, pero lo olvidé_ — I was going to do it, but I forgot it
