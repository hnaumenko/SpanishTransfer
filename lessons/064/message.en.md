🇪🇸 *Lesson 64 — Language Transfer*

*Main idea:*
You've now learned the full picture of the point\-in\-the\-past tense for all persons\. For _\-ar_ verbs, you use _\-é_, _\-ó_, _\-amos_, _\-aste_, _\-aron_\. For _\-er/\-ir_ verbs, you use _\-í_, _\-ió_, _\-imos_, _\-iste_, _\-ieron_\. Notice how the _\-er/\-ir_ group in the past is dominated by the _i_ sound everywhere except _\-imos_ — the exact opposite of the present tense, where _e_ dominates and _i_ only appears in _\-imos_\.

*New constructions:*
This lesson reviews and completes the preterite \(point\-in\-the\-past\) conjugation pattern rather than introducing individual new words\. The focus is on the _you\-informal_ and _they_ forms: _\-aste_ and _\-aron_ for _\-ar_ verbs, _\-iste_ and _\-ieron_ for _\-er/\-ir_ verbs\. The teacher emphasizes recognizing the inverse relationship between vowel patterns in the present versus past tenses\.

*Remember:*
Spanish uses _hace_ \(it makes\) to express _ago_ — so _hace dos semanas_ means literally "it makes two weeks," which we translate as "two weeks ago\."

*Examples:*
\- _¿Hablaste?_ — Did you speak?
\- _¿Comiste?_ — Did you eat?
\- _¿No saliste anoche?_ — Didn't you go out last night?
\- _¿Cuándo llegaste?_ — When did you arrive?
\- _Hablaron_ — They spoke
\- _Esperaron_ — They waited
\- _Comieron_ — They ate
\- _Le respondí hace un mes_ — I responded to her one month ago
