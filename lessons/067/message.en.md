🇪🇸 *Lesson 67 — Language Transfer*

*Key pattern:*
Spanish has clear rules for where the stress falls in a word\. If a word ends in a vowel, N, or S, the natural accent is on the second\-to\-last syllable — just like present\-tense verbs\. If it ends in any other consonant, the accent falls on the final syllable — just like infinitives\. When a word breaks these rules, you'll see a written accent mark to show you where the stress actually goes\.

*New constructions:*
This lesson teaches the accent and stress rules in Spanish rather than individual vocabulary\. The pattern: vowel/N/S endings stress the penultimate syllable; other consonants stress the final syllable\. Written accents mark exceptions\. Some accents also differentiate identical words \(_sí_ vs\. _si_, _tú_ vs\. _tu_\) or question words from non\-question words \(_dónde_ vs\. _donde_\)\.

*Remember:*
You can always look back at present\-tense verbs to remember the default accent rule — they end in vowels, N, or S, and the stress naturally falls on the second\-to\-last syllable without needing a written accent\.

*Examples:*
\- _Encuentro_ — I encounter \(no written accent; ends in vowel, stress on second\-to\-last syllable\)
\- _Encontró_ — He found \(written accent; ends in vowel but stress is on the last syllable\)
\- _Vivió_ — She lived \(written accent; breaks the vowel rule\)
\- _Limón_ — Lemon \(written accent; ends in N but stress is on the last syllable\)
\- _Salud_ — Health \(no written accent; ends in D, so stress naturally falls on the end\)
\- _Tradición_ — Tradition \(written accent; ends in N but stress is on the final syllable\)
\- _Hablamos_ — We speak \(no written accent; follows the natural rule\)
