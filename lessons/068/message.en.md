🇪🇸 *Lesson 68 — Language Transfer*

*Main idea:*
Spanish has different forms for "this" and "that" depending on what they point to\. When they're used with a masculine or feminine noun, they match the gender: _este hombre_, _esa noche_\. But when you're saying "this" or "that" generally — not pointing at a specific noun — you use a neutral form: _esto_ \(this\) or _eso_ \(that\)\. These neutral forms don't change\.

*New constructions:*
\- _este_ — this \(masculine\)
\- _esta_ — this \(feminine\)
\- _estos_ — these \(masculine\)
\- _estas_ — these \(feminine\)
\- _ese_ — that \(masculine\)
\- _esa_ — that \(feminine\)
\- _esos_ — those \(masculine\)
\- _esas_ — those \(feminine\)
\- _esto_ — this \(neutral, not referring to a specific noun\)
\- _eso_ — that \(neutral, not referring to a specific noun\)
\- _hombre_ — man
\- _tan_ — so \(as in "so tired"\)
\- _el día_ — the day \(masculine noun ending in \-a\)
\- _cuántas veces_ — how many times

*Remember:*
The neutral forms _esto_ and _eso_ are used when you're not referring to a specific masculine or feminine noun — like when you say "I can't believe this" without meaning a particular thing\.

*Examples:*
\- _Este hombre_ — This man
\- _Esos hombres_ — Those men
\- _No creo esto_ — I don't believe this
\- _Nada me gustó más que eso_ — Nothing pleased me more than that
\- _Estaba tan cansada_ — I was so tired
\- _Estábamos tan cansados que dormimos todo el día_ — We were so tired that we slept all day
\- _No sé cuántas veces lo visité_ — I don't know how many times I visited him
\- _No sé cuántas veces la visitaron_ — I don't know how many times they visited her
