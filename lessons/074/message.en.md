🇪🇸 *Lesson 74 — Language Transfer*

*The core rule:*
After _para_ \(meaning "in order to" or "so that"\), you must use the subjunctive mood\. When _para_ connects two different subjects — like "we cooked so that they eat" — the subjunctive is required\. If you try to shortcut it, you'll accidentally change the meaning to something about yourself instead\.

*New constructions:*
\- _elegir_ — to choose \(from "elect"\); the stressed _e_ becomes _i_: _elijo_, _eliges_
\- _romper_ — to break \(related to "rupture," "corrupt"\)
\- _encontrarse_ — to meet up, to find oneself
\- _volver_ — to return, to go back \(from "revolve"\); the stressed _o_ splits: _vuelvo_, _vuelves_
\- _hecho_ — I have done \(also means "fact"\)
\- _tan_ — so, as
\- _también_ — also, as well \(literally "as well"\)

*Remember:*
When _para_ means "in order to" and there are two different subjects in the sentence, you can't make a shortcut — the second verb must go into the subjunctive\.

*Examples:*
\- _No quiero que elijas por mí_ — I don't want you to choose for me
\- _No quiero que se rompa_ — I don't want it to break
\- _Nos hemos encontrado para hablar_ — We have met up to speak
\- _Hemos cocinado para que coman_ — We have cooked so that they eat
\- _Lo he hecho para que vuelvas_ — I have done it so that you come back
\- _Lo han hecho para que volvamos_ — They have done it so that we come back
\- _Hemos hecho todo para que tú también vuelvas_ — We have done everything so that you too come back
