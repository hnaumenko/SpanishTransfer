🇪🇸 *Lesson 76 — Language Transfer*

*Main idea:*
Spanish uses _hay_ to express both "there is" and "there are" — it doesn't change form like the English version does\. The past form is _había_, which also stays the same for both "there was" and "there were\." Both _hay_ and _había_ come from the verb _haber_, not from _ser_ or _estar_\. When you want to say "there has to be" or "there must be," you're still using this same verb, so it becomes _tiene que haber_ or _debe haber_ — not a form of _ser_ or _estar_\.

*New constructions:*
\- _hay_ — there is / there are
\- _había_ — there was / there were
\- _alguien_ — somebody
\- _haber_ — the verb from which _hay_ and _había_ come; used for existence

*Remember:*
When you say "there has to be something," the "be" part refers to existence, so Spanish uses _haber_, not _ser_ or _estar_\. Don't be tempted to change _hay_ to match singular or plural, and don't change _había_ to match "was" versus "were" — they stay the same\.

*Examples:*
\- _Hay alguien aquí que quiere hablar con usted_ — There is somebody here that wants to speak with you
\- _¿Hay alguien aquí que hable español?_ — Is there somebody here that speaks Spanish?
\- _Hay dos personas aquí que hablan español_ — There are two people here that speak Spanish
\- _Había dos personas aquí que hablaban español_ — There were two people here that spoke Spanish
\- _Tiene que haber algo que puedas hacer_ — There has to be something that you can do
