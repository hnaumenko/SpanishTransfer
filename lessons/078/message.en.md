🇪🇸 *Lesson 78 — Language Transfer*

*The core rule:*
After certain verbs like _empezar_ \(to start\), _comenzar_ \(to start\), and _terminar_ \(to finish\), you can use either the infinitive or the \-ing form — and the meaning changes\. To connect to an infinitive, these verbs need a preposition: _empezar a_ or _comenzar a_ \(with _a_\) and _terminar de_ \(with _de_\)\. But when you use the \-ing form instead, there's no preposition, and the meaning shifts to "start out" or "end up\."

*New constructions:*
\- _seguir_ — to follow, to continue, to carry on
\- _gratis_ — free \(not requiring money\)
\- _espectacular_ — spectacular
\- _empezar a_ — to begin to \(needs _a_ to connect to infinitive\)
\- _comenzar a_ — to start to \(needs _a_ to connect to infinitive\)
\- _terminar de_ — to finish \(doing something\) \(needs _de_ to connect to infinitive\)

*Remember:*
If a verb needs a preposition to connect to an infinitive, that's often a clue that using the \-ing form instead will give you a different meaning\.

*Examples:*
\- _Sigo hablando_ — I continue speaking
\- _Es probable que siga hablando_ — It's probable that he continues speaking
\- _Es imperativo que siga siendo gratis_ — It's imperative that it continues being free
\- _Es espectacular que empiecen a hablar español_ — It's spectacular that they begin to speak Spanish
\- _Es espectacular que empiecen hablando español_ — It's spectacular that they start out speaking Spanish
\- _Quiero que habléis conmigo cuando terminéis de venderlos_ — I want you guys to speak with me when you finish selling them
\- _Quiero que habléis conmigo cuando terminéis vendiéndolos_ — I want you guys to speak with me when you end up selling them
