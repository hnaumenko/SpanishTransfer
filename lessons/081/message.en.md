🇪🇸 *Lesson 81 — Language Transfer*

*What this lesson is about:*
Spanish has a past subjunctive mood, and it's built using a simple pattern\. You start with the "they" form of the preterite \(the point in the past\), change the final _\-on_ to _\-a_, and work backwards from there\. The past subjunctive works just like the present subjunctive — it shows up after the same triggers — but it refers to past time\. You use it when the main verb is in the past and you're expressing something uncertain, wanted, or hypothetical\.

*New constructions:*
\- _volver a \[infinitive\]_ — to do something again \(literally "to return to do"\)
\- _volver_ — to return, to come back
\- _puerta_ — door
\- _pasar_ — to happen, to pass
\- _suceder_ — to happen, to occur
\- _ocurrir_ — to happen, to occur
Past subjunctive formation: take the _ellos_ preterite form, change _\-on_ to _\-a_ for they, drop the _\-n_ for I/he/she/you formal, add _\-mos_ for we, add _\-is_ for you informal\.

*Remember:*
The past subjunctive is the same whether you're using the preterite or the imperfect in the main clause — there's only one past subjunctive form, not two\.

*Examples:*
\- _No quiero que la puerta se cierre_ — I don't want the door to close
\- _No quiero que vuelva a pasar_ — I don't want it to happen again
\- _Quería que comieran con nosotros_ — I wanted them to eat with us
\- _Esperaba que me llamara_ — I was hoping he would call me
\- _Necesitábamos que hablara con nosotros_ — We needed him to speak with us
\- _Te llamé otra vez para que organizáramos_ — I called you again so that we could organize
