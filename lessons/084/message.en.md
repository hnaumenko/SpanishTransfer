🇪🇸 *Lesson 84 — Language Transfer*

*Main idea:*
Spanish has a group of irregular verbs that add _go_ in the I form of the present tense\. These are called the "go verbs\." When you move to the subjunctive mood, that _go_ changes to _g_ for all persons — not just for I\. This pattern helps you predict how these irregulars behave across different tenses\.

*New constructions:*
\- _tengo_ — I have \(from _tener_\)
\- _vengo_ — I come \(from _venir_\)
\- _salgo_ — I go out, I leave \(from _salir_\)
\- _pongo_ — I put \(from _poner_\)
\- _digo_ — I say, I tell \(from _decir_\)
\- _hago_ — I do, I make \(from _hacer_\)
\- _oigo_ — I hear \(from _oír_\)
\- _traigo_ — I bring \(from _traer_\)
\- _traer_ — to bring; when part of another verb, translates as "tract"
\- _extraer_ — to extract
\- _atraer_ — to attract
\- _contraer_ — to contract
\- _distraer_ — to distract
\- _venga_ — come\! \(subjunctive/command form you'll hear in Spain as "come on\!"\)
\- _dígame_ — tell me \(formal command often heard when answering the phone in Spain\)

*Remember:*
The _go_ that appears only for the I form in the present tense spreads to all persons in the subjunctive mood, but as _g_\.

*Examples:*
\- _Tengo un coche_ — I have a car
\- _Quiero que vengas_ — I want you to come
\- _Salgo mañana_ — I leave tomorrow
\- _No quiero que me distraigas_ — I don't want you to distract me
\- _Quiero que traigas el libro_ — I want you to bring the book
